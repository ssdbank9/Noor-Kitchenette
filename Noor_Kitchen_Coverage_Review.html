Noor’s Kitchen coverage review

The earlier workflow did not capture every idea in the Excel planners. The HTML preserves the recipe catalog and several useful kitchen functions, but it has feature gaps and reproduced defects.

The fuller app should combine meal planning, pantry-based suggestions, recipe cards, shopping, actual cooking records, stock history, and recovery. The original one-week pilot is not a cap on the intended product.

What the sources establish

  • Both Excel planners have the same eight sheets, including a hidden Lists sheet. After normalizing array-formula range notation, their cell differences are the default person count, one selected recipe, and footer punctuation. Their dropdown storage differs, but the intended eleven validation rules are present in both.
  • All 22 recipe entries, 62 ingredients, and 276 recipe-ingredient rows reconcile to the HTML. Names, servings, times, notes, recipe and video hyperlink targets, ingredient units, initial stock, and minimum thresholds also reconcile.
  • The brief workflow omitted ranked suggestions, source links, recipe times and notes, minimum-stock controls, ingredient update metadata, cooking-date choices, store grouping, monthly history, and backup/recovery detail.
  • The HTML does not contain a weekly meal plan or a shopping basket derived from several planned meals. Its shopping uses low-stock thresholds and one selected dish.
  • The current photo feature displays a photo for manual edits and supports an external Claude copy/paste helper. It does not analyse the image inside the app.

Shopping needs a deliberate redesign. The Excel catalog-total column sums ingredients across all recipes. It is not the amount needed for a selected week. Preserve single-dish shortages, minimum stock, and aisle grouping; calculate the new basket from the actual selected meals and servings.

Confirmed requirements: phone with home-screen access, photo pantry updates, and manual items with quantities and weights. Shared access, recipe-photo import, budget features, and the remaining proposals have not all been approved as v1 commitments.

Expanded plain-English workflow

  1. Set up the kitchen. Choose the usual number of people, meal names, preferred units, storage locations, and solo or shared access. Review starter stock instead of accepting sample quantities as real inventory.
  2. Bring in the recipe collection. Keep the existing 22 entries, ingredient quantities, times, notes, videos, and written links. Add and edit family recipes with full steps. Photo recipe import gets its own reviewed flow.
  3. Record actual pantry stock. Add items manually or review items detected from a picture. Enter quantity and unit, storage location, and optional expiry/frozen-on date. Keep uncertain amounts visible.
  4. Decide or plan. Today shows ready recipes and the best near matches. Noor can cook spontaneously or assign recipes and servings to dates and meal slots.
  5. Build the shopping basket. Combine the selected meals, subtract available stock once, and add chosen minimum-stock top-ups. Group by aisle and show dish-specific shortages and editable store hints.
  6. Record purchases. Tick what was bought, confirm the actual quantity and optional price, and update stock once. Link a receipt and grocery photo if they describe the same purchase.
  7. Cook and confirm usage. Open the scaled recipe, instructions, notes, or video. Record the actual cooking date, meal type, servings, and ingredient amounts used. Then update stock and history together.
  8. Handle the rest of kitchen life. Record chai ingredients and other everyday usage, remaining portions, food moved to the freezer, finished items, and waste. Corrections can be undone without corrupting history.
  9. Review and protect the records. See monthly meal and dish summaries, servings prepared, stock movements, and grocery spending where prices were captured. Share only on user action and support validated export and restore.

Chicken - 500 g saves quantity 500 in the ingredient’s kg unit. Negative -2 is parsed as positive 2.

Correction: Parse and validate quantity and unit together. Reject invalid input and review the converted amount.

Chicken stock goes 1000 → 999.3333 → 1000, while cooking history still contains one meal and Stats still counts it.

Verification and limits

Inspected all sixteen worksheets, including hidden sheets, formulas, cached values, hyperlinks, comments, dropdown XML, and all HTML source. Compared source catalog contents and ran 10 focused browser checks in headless Chrome at 390 × 844 pixels, with Asia/Karachi timezone and disposable data. No browser verification errors were recorded. This does not mean that the reproduced product defects passed.

  • No native Excel recalculation was performed. Formula text, cached values, raw XML dropdowns, hidden content, and relationships were inspected.
  • Browser checks used disposable isolated data and local files. No real household data or original source file was changed.
  • Physical Android/iPhone camera capture, home-screen installation, offline synchronization, cloud vision, multi-user access, and external sharing delivery were not tested.
  • Recipe/video URLs and store suggestions were preserved but their current availability, accuracy, ranking, prices, and delivery claims were not verified.
  • A valid backup/restore was tested; malformed restore payloads were not tested.
